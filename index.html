A browser that stays out of the way.

No telemetry calling home, no bundled Google services, no ad platform baked into the settings page. Built on Chromium, stripped of what doesn't belong to you.

Named after the lightest solid there is.

Aerium takes its name from aerogel — a material that's up to 99% air by volume, solid enough to hold its shape, light enough to rest on a flower without bending the stem. That's the standard the browser is held to: strip away the weight that doesn't need to be there, keep what actually works.

Built by Dioide on top of Chromium — using ungoogled-chromium on Windows and Linux, and a Vanadium-based fork on Android — with a curated set of privacy defaults and a browser identity of its own, not a reskin.

What you get

The same principles across every platform, adapted to what each one actually supports.

No phoning home

No telemetry, no bundled Google services, no background pings you didn't ask for. Updates are something you check for, not something the browser does quietly on its own.

Extensions that work

The Chrome Web Store is available from the first launch, Manifest V2 included on Android — install and update the same way you would anywhere else.

Search without a middleman

Startpage is the default engine, with DuckDuckGo, DuckDuckGo Lite, DuckDuckGo HTML, and SearXNG ready to pick from Settings.

HTTPS by default

Balanced Mode upgrades navigations to HTTPS automatically, without the disruptive full-site warnings of strict HTTPS-only enforcement.

Global Privacy Control

The Sec-GPC opt-out signal and navigator.globalPrivacyControl — recognized under CCPA, still missing from stock Chromium — are sent on every page, no toggle needed.

Privacy flags, visible not silent

Fingerprinting resistance, reduced system info, and more are one click away in chrome://flags — listed plainly on first run instead of switched on for you without asking.

Your own colors

Its own name, its own icon — the appearance picker in Settings works exactly like stock Chromium. Nothing forces a palette on top of it.

DRM, your call

Widevine isn't registered unless you turn it on yourself at chrome://flags/#enable-widevine. No silent probing for it on every DRM-gated site.
